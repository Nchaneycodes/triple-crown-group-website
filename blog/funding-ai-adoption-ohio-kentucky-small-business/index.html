Funding AI Adoption: Ohio and Kentucky Programs Worth Knowing About

Quick answer: There is no AI-specific grant program in Ohio or Kentucky, but several general programs apply. The most realistic path for most businesses is workforce training reimbursement, AI enablement for a sales or marketing team is legitimately workforce training, and training programs are easier to qualify for than technology grants aimed at product development. Ohio offers reimbursement up to $2,000 per employee credential for technology training. Verify all current terms directly with the administering agency, because these programs change.

A note before anything else: chasing funding is a common way to distort a good plan into a fundable one. Decide what you should do first, then look for what offsets it. Programs that require you to reshape the project are usually not worth the reshaping.

The categories that actually apply

1. Workforce training reimbursement (most realistic)

Why this fits AI adoption best: what you are actually buying (enablement, training a team on new tools and workflows, building internal capability) is workforce training under most program definitions. Training programs are also generally less competitive and less administratively heavy than technology grants.

Ohio: employers can receive reimbursement of up to $2,000 per employee credential for technology-focused training. For a 20-person team, that is meaningful against a $10,000 to $15,000 training program.

Kentucky: the state operates workforce training programs through its economic development apparatus that can apply to technology skills training.

What to check: eligibility requirements, whether the training provider must be approved in advance, whether credentials are required versus general training, and the application timing relative to when training occurs. That last one matters, several programs require approval before training begins, so applying after the fact disqualifies you.

2. Small business development grants

Ohio operates several categories, including Spark grants with typical totals in the $50,000 to $100,000 range and Accelerant grants in the $50,000 to $250,000 range. Ohio also has programs specifically targeting micro-businesses (five or fewer employees) offering training, matched savings, and expansion support, with an emphasis on low-to-moderate-income communities.

What to check: these programs typically have specific use restrictions, geographic or demographic eligibility criteria, and competitive application processes. Read the allowable use list carefully before investing time.

3. SBIR / STTR and state matching (for technology developers only)

The federal Small Business Innovation Research and Small Business Technology Transfer programs represent the largest source of non-dilutive funding for technology-driven small businesses, with eleven federal agencies collectively awarding over $4 billion annually. Kentucky operates a matching program through the Kentucky Science and Technology Corporation that adds state funds to federal awards.

The important distinction: these programs fund companies developing technology, not companies adopting it. If you are building an AI product, this is a serious option worth real effort. If you are a distributor implementing a quoting agent, it is not a fit and pursuing it will waste months.

4. Manufacturing extension services

For manufacturers, the manufacturing extension partnership network operates in both states, providing assessments and technology adoption support at subsidized rates. Not a grant, but effectively reduced-cost expertise, and underused by small and mid-sized manufacturers.

5. Federal small business resources

SBA-affiliated resources, Small Business Development Centers, and SCORE offer free or low-cost advisory support. Not funding, but genuinely useful for a business trying to think through the decision before spending, and free.

How to think about it strategically

Sequence matters. Decide what you should do based on your business, then look for offsets. Reverse order produces projects designed around eligibility criteria rather than around outcomes.

Training is the easiest fit. If you are going to spend on AI, the training and enablement portion is the most likely to qualify for reimbursement. Structure your engagement so the training component is clearly delineated and documented as such, a separate line item with defined hours and outcomes.

Timing usually requires pre-approval. Most training reimbursement programs require application before the training occurs. Build a 4 to 8 week lead time into your planning.

Documentation requirements are real. Attendance records, curriculum documentation, credential evidence, invoices. Ask your provider whether they can supply what the program requires before you commit, many cannot.

Do not let it delay you six months. If a program adds two months and $8,000 of offset to a $30,000 project, that may be worth it. If it adds six months, the delay costs more than the grant.

Where to verify current information

These programs change terms, funding levels, and eligibility regularly. Anything written about them ages quickly, including this post. Verify current details with:

  • Ohio: the Ohio Department of Development and its business assistance resources
  • Kentucky: the Kentucky Cabinet for Economic Development and the Kentucky Science and Technology Corporation
  • Regional: REDI Cincinnati and BE NKY Growth Partnership, both of which track available programs and will point you at what fits
  • Federal: SBA and your local Small Business Development Center

The regional economic development organizations are the best first call. They know what is currently available, they know the administering staff, and the conversation is free.

Frequently asked questions

Are there grants for AI adoption in Ohio? No AI-specific program, but technology training reimbursement and general small business development grants can apply. Ohio offers up to $2,000 per employee credential for technology-focused training.

Can Kentucky businesses get funding for AI? Kentucky participates in SBIR/STTR with a state matching program through KSTC, which fits technology developers. Workforce training programs are the better fit for businesses adopting existing tools.

What is the most realistic source? Workforce training reimbursement. AI enablement is legitimately workforce training, and training programs are less competitive than technology grants.

Do I need a grant writer? For training reimbursement, generally no. The applications are manageable internally. For competitive grants above $50,000, a grant writer or your economic development organization's assistance is usually worth it.

Should funding availability change what we do? It should change the sequence and the structure, not the substance. If a program funds training but not builds, do the training first. Do not build a project you would not otherwise want because it is fundable.
