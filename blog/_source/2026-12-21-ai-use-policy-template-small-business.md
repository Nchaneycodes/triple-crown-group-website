**Quick answer:** An effective AI use policy covers four things, approved tools and how to request new ones, data that may never be entered, output requiring human review, and who to ask. Two pages. Longer policies do not get read, and a policy nobody reads does not change behavior. Below is a template you can adapt.

Most small businesses do not have one of these, and their employees have been using AI tools with company data for a year or more. That is the actual situation this document addresses.

## Before you adapt the template

Three things to decide first:

**Which tools are approved.** Name them specifically. Business or enterprise tier, not consumer free tier.

**What your sensitive data actually is.** This differs by business. A manufacturer's sensitive data is drawings and pricing. A staffing firm's is candidate resumes. A healthcare adjacent business has patient information. Be specific to yours rather than generic.

**Who owns this.** One name. Someone who can answer questions within a day.

## The template

*Adapt the bracketed sections. Replace the examples with your own.*

---

### [Company Name], AI Use Policy

**Effective [date] · Owner: [name, title, email] · Review: annually**

#### 1. Purpose

AI tools can make our work faster and better. They also create risks around confidentiality, accuracy, and quality. This policy explains what tools we use, what information may not go into them, and what has to be reviewed before it leaves the company.

This policy applies to all employees and contractors.

#### 2. Approved tools

The following tools are approved for business use:

- **[Tool name]**. [what it is used for]
- **[Tool name]**. [what it is used for]

You must use your company account. Do not use personal accounts or free consumer versions for company work.

**To request a new tool:** email [owner]. You will get an answer within [10 business days]. Do not begin using an unapproved tool with company information while a request is pending.

#### 3. Information that must never be entered into AI tools

Do not enter any of the following into any AI tool:

- Customer or client personal information, names combined with contact details, account numbers, or personal circumstances
- [Health information / patient information] *(include if applicable)*
- Financial account details, payment card information, or banking information
- Passwords, API keys, credentials, or access tokens
- Anything covered by a confidentiality agreement or NDA
- Unreleased financial results or other material non-public information
- Contract terms, negotiated pricing, or rate agreements
- Employee records, performance information, or compensation data
- [Proprietary designs, drawings, specifications, or source code] *(include if applicable)*
- [Add anything specific to your business]

**A simple test:** if this information appearing in a competitor's inbox would be a problem, do not put it in an AI tool.

**If you are unsure, ask [owner] before entering it.** Asking is always the right call and nobody will be criticized for it.

#### 4. Review requirements

**Nothing generated by AI leaves this company without a person reading it first.** This includes:

- Emails and messages to customers, prospects, or vendors
- Proposals, quotes, and contracts
- Published content of any kind
- Reports provided to customers
- [Anything else specific to your business]

You are responsible for anything you send, regardless of what produced the first draft.

**Verify facts.** AI tools produce confident, plausible information that is sometimes wrong. Any number, name, date, citation, or factual claim must be verified before it goes out.

#### 5. Quality expectations

AI is a drafting and research assistant, not a replacement for your judgment.

- Edit AI output substantively. Do not send first drafts.
- Do not use AI to produce work you do not understand.
- Do not use AI to make decisions that require judgment about people, pricing, or commitments.

#### 6. What AI must not be used for

- Making decisions about hiring, promotion, discipline, or termination without human judgment and review
- Producing [legal advice / medical advice / financial advice / coverage determinations] *(adapt to your business)*
- Generating anything represented as human-created where that distinction matters to the recipient
- Any use that would violate a customer agreement, regulation, or professional obligation that applies to us

#### 7. Questions

Contact [name, email]. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask before doing it.

#### 8. Acknowledgment

I have read and understand this policy.

Name: ________________ Date: ________

## After you adopt it

**Distribute and collect acknowledgments.** A policy nobody signed is a document, not a policy.

**Cover it in a 20-minute session.** Reading a document produces less understanding than five minutes of examples. Use real scenarios from your business: "Can I paste this customer email in? What about this quote?"

**Add it to onboarding.** New hires get it in week one.

**Review annually,** and immediately whenever a vendor materially changes their terms.

**Make the approved path easy.** If getting a tool approved takes three months, people will not wait. The request process in section 2 is the part that keeps the rest of the policy honest.

## Sector-specific additions

**Healthcare-adjacent:** add PHI handling requirements, business associate agreement status for each tool, and privacy officer sign-off on new workflows.

**Financial services:** add records retention alignment, supervision procedures, and communication rule review for anything client-facing.

**Legal:** add citation verification as a hard requirement, client confidentiality specifics, and jurisdiction-specific bar guidance.

**Insurance:** add licensed producer review for anything constituting coverage advice.

**Staffing and recruiting:** add the screening boundary, AI may surface and organize candidates, humans make all advance and reject decisions.

**Agencies and consultancies:** add a requirement to check client master service agreements for AI restrictions before using client data in any tool.

## A note on scope

This is a general template for small and mid-sized businesses. It is not legal advice. If you operate in a regulated industry, handle sensitive customer data, or have contractual confidentiality obligations, have counsel review your adapted version. That review is usually inexpensive and worth it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should an AI use policy include?**
Approved tools and a request process, prohibited data, review requirements, and who to ask. Two pages.

**Does a small business really need one?**
Yes, urgently, because unmanaged usage with company data is already happening.

**Can I just copy a template?**
Start from one and adapt it. Adjust the prohibited data list to your business, name your actual tools, and get counsel review if you are regulated.

**What if someone violates it?**
Handle it as a training issue first. Most violations are people trying to do their jobs with a useful tool. Repeated or deliberate violations involving sensitive data are a different conversation.

**Should we prohibit AI instead?**
No. Prohibition moves usage to personal devices where you have no visibility and no control. A sanctioned path with clear boundaries is the safer arrangement.
