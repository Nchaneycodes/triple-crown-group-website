These companies have something Covington's small firms do not: an actual marketing department, with specialists, a budget, and existing systems.

That changes the problem. The constraint is not capacity to start. It is that five people are each experimenting independently and nothing is compounding.

## The mid-market marketing problem

At 8 to 25 marketing people, the pattern is consistent:

- Two or three people are using AI heavily and effectively
- Three or four have tried it and drifted off
- Nobody has documented anything
- Output quality varies visibly across the team
- Nobody knows what anyone is putting into which tool
- Leadership has no idea whether any of it is working

This is not an adoption problem. It is a **standardization** problem, and it needs a different intervention than a small company's "get started" problem or an enterprise's governance problem.

## Build order for a mid-market marketing team

### 1. Brand voice guide (weeks 1 to 3)

**Why first:** everything downstream depends on it. Scaling content production without a documented voice produces volume that reads generic, and generic content from a company with an established brand is actively damaging.

**How to build it:** collect your five strongest pieces, have AI analyze them for structure, vocabulary, and rhetorical patterns, then edit that analysis into a two-page guide. Add a ban list, specific words and constructions your brand does not use. That ban list is the most useful part and it is what everyone skips.

**Cost:** $2,500 to $5,000, or free if someone internal has a day.

### 2. Content production workflow (weeks 4 to 8)

**The workflow that works:** human sets the point of view, AI does research, human writes the opening, AI expands, human does a substantive edit.

**What to document:** the prompt set, the voice guide inclusion, the review standard, and who reviews what. Package the prompts as one-click custom assistants so people are not pasting configurations.

**The quality standard to write down:** publish nothing that does not contain at least one thing only your company could have written, a client story, your own data, a specific position.

**Cost:** $6,000 to $14,000 including training.

### 3. Reporting automation (weeks 8 to 12)

**Why it lands well:** monthly and quarterly reporting is high-volume, structured, universally disliked, and usually consuming a specialist's week every month. It is also the win most visible to leadership, which matters for continued funding.

**What it does:** turns your analytics exports into narrative reports (what changed, likely drivers, what to do about it) in your format.

**Cost:** $6,000 to $15,000.

### 4. Campaign and research support (quarter 2)

Competitive analysis, audience research, campaign concepting, and message testing. Lower urgency, real value, easier once the team has the habits.

## The governance layer mid-market companies need

Small companies can operate on trust. Enterprises have compliance functions. Mid-market companies are in between and usually have nothing, which is where the exposure sits.

**The minimum:**

- **A two-page AI use policy.** Approved tools, prohibited data, review requirements, who to ask.
- **Business-tier licenses for everyone.** Not just heavy users, unlicensed people use personal accounts, which is the actual risk.
- **Admin controls configured.** Training disabled, retention set, SSO, and offboarding that removes AI access.
- **A named owner.** One person in marketing operations who maintains the prompt library and answers questions. Roughly 20% of a role.

None of this is expensive. All of it is missing at most mid-market companies.

## Measuring it

Three numbers at 90 days:

1. **Content output per person**, or cycle time per piece
2. **Reporting hours per month**, the cleanest number you will get
3. **Adoption**: percentage of the team using the documented workflow without reminders

And one qualitative check that matters more than it should: read three recent pieces aloud. If they sound like your company, the voice guide is working. If they sound like anyone's company, it is not.
