**Quick answer:** Insurance agencies get the most from AI in four places, commercial submission preparation, renewal review and proposal drafting, coverage comparison summaries, and client communication. The hard boundary: AI drafts, a licensed producer reviews everything client-facing. No coverage advice, no binding quotes, no statements about what is covered without a licensed human in the loop.

Greater Cincinnati has a substantial insurance presence, carriers, independent agencies, and commercial brokerages of every size. Agency work is document-heavy, deadline-driven, and highly repetitive, which is close to an ideal profile for AI. It is also regulated, which means the boundaries matter more than the capabilities.

## The four use cases

### 1. Commercial submission preparation

**The problem:** getting a commercial account to market means assembling loss runs, applications, supplemental questionnaires, and a narrative describing the risk, then sending variations to eight or more carriers. A single mid-market submission can consume most of a day.

**What AI does:** drafts the risk narrative from the application and prior submissions, adapts the package for each carrier's known appetite and format, and assembles the supporting documentation checklist.

**What it's worth:** this is the highest-return use case for commercial brokers. More submissions per producer means more markets per account, which means better placements.

**The boundary:** the AI drafts the narrative. A licensed producer reviews every word before it goes to a carrier, because a mischaracterized risk creates E&O exposure.

### 2. Renewal review and proposal generation

**The problem:** renewal season is a wall. Every account needs a review of expiring coverage, exposure changes, market conditions, and a client-facing proposal. The work is nearly identical account to account and it all lands at once.

**What AI does:** produces a first-draft renewal review from the expiring policy data and exposure changes, plus a client-facing summary explaining what changed and why.

**What it's worth:** this is the fastest measurable win because it targets the busiest weeks of the year. An agency that can put out clean renewal proposals two weeks earlier gets more client conversations before decisions are made.

### 3. Coverage comparison summaries

**The problem:** comparing quotes across carriers means reading dense policy language and identifying meaningful differences in coverage, exclusions, sublimits, and endorsements. It is slow and error-prone under time pressure.

**What AI does:** produces a structured comparison of terms across policies, highlighting differences and flagging items requiring producer attention.

**The boundary. This is the important one:** the output is an internal working document for a licensed producer, not a client deliverable and not a recommendation. AI can identify that Policy A has a $25,000 sublimit where Policy B has $100,000. It must not conclude which is better for the client. That determination is licensed advice, and it stays with the producer.

Build this constraint into the tooling itself: the output format should be a difference table, not a recommendation.

### 4. Client communication and service

**The problem:** certificate requests, coverage questions, claims status updates, and routine service correspondence consume enormous account manager time and are largely repetitive.

**What AI does:** drafts responses to routine service requests from your existing documentation and account records.

**The boundary:** any response that states what is covered goes through a licensed reviewer. Purely administrative responses (certificate timing, document requests, scheduling) carry less risk. Draw the line explicitly in writing rather than leaving it to individual judgment.

## The compliance frame

State insurance regulation attaches responsibility to licensed individuals. That principle organizes everything:

**AI can do:** summarize, draft, compare, extract, organize, format.

**AI cannot do:** advise, recommend coverage, bind, quote, or make any statement to a client about what is covered without licensed review.

**Practical requirements:**

- A written AI use policy naming what may and may not be done, distributed to all staff
- Documented review by a licensed producer on all client-facing output
- Clear rules about what client data may enter which tools, insurance agencies hold health, financial, and personal data with real obligations attached
- Business-tier tools with contractual data handling commitments; no consumer free tiers with client data, ever
- Retention alignment with your existing record-keeping obligations

Get your E&O carrier's view before rollout. Some carriers have started asking about AI use in applications, and a documented policy is a better answer than an improvised one.

## What it looks like by agency size

**Small agency, 5 to 15 people.** General assistant licenses, a documented prompt set for renewals and service correspondence, a written policy, and a training session. **$5,000 to $12,000** in year one.

**Mid-sized agency, 25 to 75 people.** The above plus a custom submission or renewal agent grounded in your templates and carrier appetites, plus AMS integration. **$25,000 to $60,000** in year one.

**Commercial brokerage with a benefits practice.** Add coverage comparison tooling and a stricter governance layer given the health data involved. **$45,000 to $100,000**.

## The sequencing that works

**Phase 1, Policy and training.** Before any tooling. Staff need to know the boundaries before they start experimenting, because they are already experimenting whether or not you have authorized it. This is genuinely urgent at most agencies.

**Phase 2, Renewal drafting.** Highest-return, well-bounded, immediately visible during renewal season.

**Phase 3, Submission preparation.** Requires more setup around carrier appetites and templates.

**Phase 4, Service correspondence and comparison tooling.** Highest compliance sensitivity, so do it last with the most governance attention.

## Frequently asked questions

**How can insurance agencies use AI?**
Submission preparation, renewal review and proposals, coverage comparison summaries, and client communication drafting, with licensed review on everything client-facing.

**Is AI compliant for insurance sales?**
Yes as a drafting and analysis assistant with licensed review. Not for coverage advice, recommendations, or statements of coverage produced without a licensed person in the loop.

**What is the fastest win?**
Renewal preparation. High volume, deadline-driven, repetitive, and concentrated in the year's busiest weeks.

**Can AI read policy documents?**
Yes, and it is good at extracting terms, limits, exclusions, and endorsements into structured summaries. Treat the output as a working document for a licensed reviewer, not as a determination.

**What about our AMS?**
Most agency management systems are adding AI features. Check what your vendor already offers before buying externally. Integration with your AMS is what makes outputs specific to your accounts rather than generic, so it is worth scoping properly.

**Do we need to disclose AI use to clients?**
Disclosure requirements vary and are evolving. The defensible position: a licensed producer reviews and takes responsibility for all client-facing output, which is what the client is paying for regardless of what drafted the first version. Discuss specifics with your compliance counsel.
