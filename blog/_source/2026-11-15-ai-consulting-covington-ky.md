**Quick answer:** For most Covington businesses (which skew small, professional, and service-based) the right first move is not a custom build. It is getting everyone onto a business-tier assistant, writing a two-page use policy, and standardizing one repeated weekly workflow. A practical first engagement for a firm under 20 people runs $5,000 to $12,000.

Covington's business base is different from Florence's or Blue Ash's. It is denser in small professional services, creative firms, agencies, and B2B companies operating from riverfront and MainStrasse-area offices, alongside larger corporate presences. Small teams, high service intensity, thin operational overhead.

That profile changes what "AI implementation" should mean.

## Why small professional firms need a different approach

A 12-person firm does not have a RevOps function, a marketing operations person, or anyone whose job includes evaluating software. The partner or owner is also the salesperson, and the constraint is not knowledge. It is that nobody has an uninterrupted afternoon.

Three consequences:

**Custom builds are usually wrong.** The volume is not there to amortize a $20,000 agent. Configuration and training deliver nearly all the value at a fraction of the cost.

**The bottleneck is standardization, not capability.** In most small firms, one or two people are already using AI effectively and nobody else knows what they are doing. Documenting and spreading that is the whole project.

**Adoption is easy if the owner adopts.** In a 12-person firm, the owner using something visibly is the entire change management program. This is the one place small firms have a real advantage over larger companies.

## The first-90-days sequence for a small Covington firm

**Week 1. Find out what is already happening.** Ask everyone, without judgment, what AI tools they use and for what. You will find people are further along than you expected and using personal accounts with client information. Both are useful to know.

**Week 2, Business-tier licenses and a two-page policy.** Roughly $25 to $30 per person per month. The policy covers approved tools, what data never goes in, what requires review, and who to ask. Two pages, distributed, acknowledged.

**Week 3, Pick one workflow.** For most professional services firms in this market, the highest-value candidate is one of:

- **Client communication drafting**, status updates, proposals, scoping documents, recurring correspondence
- **Prospect and referral research**, briefs before a meeting, a pitch, or a networking conversation
- **Internal documentation**, meeting notes, process documentation, project summaries

Pick the one your team complains about.

**Weeks 4 to 6, Build the prompts by using them.** Not in a planning meeting. Three or four people use AI on that workflow for two weeks, then everyone compares what worked. The prompts that emerge from use are better than any written from theory, and they are owned by the people who wrote them.

**Week 7, A two-hour working session.** Everyone in a room, on real work, finalizing the prompt set. This is where standardization happens.

**Weeks 8 to 12, Use it, then check.** At week 12, ask two questions: is everyone still using it, and has anyone modified the prompts to suit themselves? Yes to both means it took.

For firms wanting to do it entirely themselves, the licensing is the only hard cost. The sequence above is not secret and a motivated owner can run it. The reason to bring someone in is speed and the fact that most owners do not, in fact, have the uninterrupted afternoons.

## When a Covington business should do more

Move past this baseline when:

- You are over roughly 25 people and workflows have started diverging across teams
- A specific repeated workflow happens more than 100 times a month
- Your client data handling requirements are stricter than the general case
- You are selling into buyers who are asking about your AI practices

Until then, the baseline is genuinely sufficient, and firms that skip ahead to custom builds usually end up with something impressive that three people use.
