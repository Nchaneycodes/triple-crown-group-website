**Quick answer:** A workable 90-day plan covers exactly one workflow. Weeks 1 to 3: assess and baseline. Weeks 4 to 6: policy, tools, and a manager-first pilot. Weeks 7 to 10: team rollout with hands-on training. Weeks 11 to 13: reinforcement and measurement. Budget $20,000 to $45,000 all-in for a 50-person organization. The most common failure is attempting five workflows and completing none.

This is the plan, laid out week by week, for a company with roughly 30 sales people and 20 marketing people.

## Before week 1: the two decisions that gate everything

**Who owns this?** One named person with at least 8 hours a week protected. Not a committee. If nobody has the capacity, fix that before starting. An ownerless initiative will consume budget and produce nothing.

**Which executive is visibly behind it?** Not just approving budget. Attending training, referencing it in meetings, asking about it. If the answer is "nobody has time for that," the outcome is already determined.

## Weeks 1 to 3: Assessment and baseline

**Week 1. Where do the hours go?**

- Time logs from 8 to 10 people across roles for one full week. Real logs, not recalled estimates, which are off by 30 to 50%.
- Shadow 4 to 6 people for two hours each. Watch, do not ask.
- Inventory every tool, every license, and actual utilization. Dormant seats are recoverable spend.

**Week 2, Data and systems readiness.**

- CRM audit: duplicate rate, field completion, note quality, activity logging consistency.
- Marketing systems: list hygiene, attribution setup, content inventory.
- What is realistically integrable, and what is not.

**Week 3, Prioritize and baseline.**

- Score opportunities on volume, repeatability, AI fit, and change difficulty. Weight change difficulty heavily.
- **Pick one.** Two at most, and only if they serve different teams.
- Write down the baseline number and the exact measurement method. This is the step most often skipped and most regretted.

**Deliverable:** a one-page decision, the workflow, the current number, the target number, the measurement method, and the owner.

**Typical spend:** $4,000 to $8,000 if consultant-supported.

## Weeks 4 to 6: Policy, tools, and manager pilot

**Week 4, AI use policy.**

Two pages, not twenty. It needs to cover:

- Approved tools and how to get access
- What data may never be entered (customer PII, contract terms, credentials, anything under NDA)
- Required human review before anything customer-facing
- Who to ask when unsure

Distribute it and require acknowledgment. Note that your team is already using AI tools informally; the policy is catching up to reality, not getting ahead of it.

**Week 5, Tool decision and configuration.**

Default to the assistant bundled with your existing suite. Configure admin controls, data retention, and access. Purchase only the seats needed for the pilot plus the first rollout cohort, not the whole company.

**Week 6, Manager pilot begins.**

Managers and one respected senior rep use the workflow. Nobody else. They break it, find the edge cases, and improve the prompt. The prompt library gets built here, from use rather than from theory.

**Typical spend:** $6,000 to $14,000 including initial licensing.

## Weeks 7 to 10: Rollout and training

**Week 7, Pilot review and prompt finalization.**

Managers report what worked and what did not. Prompts are finalized. The removal is decided: what existing task goes away when this arrives.

**Week 8, Cohort 1 training.**

- 12 to 15 people, in person, 2.5 hours
- Manager in the room and participating
- Every person completes the workflow once on their own real account
- The retired process is announced dead, by the manager, in this session
- Everyone leaves with the prompt library

**Week 9, Cohort 1 practice, cohort 2 training.**

Cohort 1 uses the workflow with support available. Cohort 2 trains, with an improved version of the material based on what cohort 1 struggled with.

**Week 10, Remaining cohorts and first usage check.**

Pull usage data for cohort 1 at their two-week mark. Anyone who has stopped gets an individual conversation, diagnostic, not disciplinary. They usually have one specific fixable blocker.

**Typical spend:** $8,000 to $18,000.

## Weeks 11 to 13: Reinforcement and measurement

**Week 11, The reinforcement week.**

This is where pilots die and where nobody schedules anything. Usage drops after novelty. Plan for it:

- Manager references the workflow output in every one-on-one and pipeline review
- Session 2 with each cohort: what broke, prompt improvements, live troubleshooting
- Individual follow-up with everyone below threshold usage

**Week 12, Measure.**

- Re-measure the baseline metric, same method, same people
- Pull adoption rate: percentage of eligible events where the workflow was used
- Check whether anyone has customized the workflow, the strongest ownership signal available

**Week 13, Decide and communicate.**

Make the call out loud to the whole team:

- **Expand.** It worked. It is standard practice now, and the language changes from "pilot" to "how we do this."
- **Adjust.** Partial results, known cause, one more 30-day cycle with a specific change.
- **Kill it.** Say so plainly, say what was learned, remove the licenses.

Then: document it for new-hire onboarding, so it becomes permanent rather than dependent on the people who were there.

**Typical spend:** $4,000 to $8,000.

## The budget, assembled

| Component | Range |
|---|---|
| Assessment and baseline | $4,000 to $8,000 |
| Policy, tools, pilot | $6,000 to $14,000 |
| Training and rollout | $8,000 to $18,000 |
| Reinforcement and measurement | $4,000 to $8,000 |
| Licensing (50 seats, 3 months) | $3,000 to $4,500 |
| **Total** | **$25,000 to $52,500** |

Plus internal time: roughly 150 to 200 hours across the organization, which at fully loaded cost is another $9,000 to $12,000 that belongs in your ROI denominator.

## What quarter two looks like

If quarter one worked, quarter two is easier and cheaper because trust exists:

- Workflow two, using the same sequence but compressed to about 8 weeks
- Data cleanup running in the background
- Internal owner running more of it, consultant on lighter support
- New-hire onboarding includes workflow one as standard

If quarter one did not work, do not add a second workflow. Find out why. Adding scope to a failing initiative is the most reliable way to make it fail permanently.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a 90-day AI plan include?**
Assessment and baseline, policy and tool decisions, a manager-first pilot, cohort training, reinforcement, and measurement, for one workflow.

**How much should a 50-person org budget?**
$25,000 to $52,500 including licensing, plus 150 to 200 hours of internal time.

**Can we go faster?**
You can compress assessment. You cannot compress adoption. Behavior change has a floor of roughly 6 to 8 weeks from training to durable habit, and rushing it produces a relaunch, which is slower than doing it once.

**What if we want to do three workflows?**
Do one. Organizations that deploy one workflow well in 90 days are further ahead at month twelve than those that attempt five, because the first group builds trust and the second builds fatigue.

**Do we need a consultant for this?**
No, if you have someone internal with the time, the credibility, and prior experience running a change program. Yes, if this is the first one and the internal owner is learning the plan and executing it simultaneously.
