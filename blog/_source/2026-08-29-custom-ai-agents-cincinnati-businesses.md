**Quick answer:** A custom AI agent is a purpose-built assistant configured for one repeated workflow and grounded in your own data. In the Cincinnati market, a focused single-workflow agent runs **$8,000 to $25,000** to build plus **$200 to $1,500/month** to operate. Build one only when the workflow is high-volume, specific to your business, and dependent on data no off-the-shelf tool has.

"Agent" has become a marketing word, which makes it hard to evaluate. Here is the practical definition and the honest economics.

## What separates an agent from a prompt

A general assistant is a blank room. Every time you use it, you explain the situation, provide the context, and describe the format you want. That works fine for occasional tasks and is exhausting for daily ones.

A custom agent is that same capability with the explanation baked in:

- **Grounded in your data.** Your catalog, your pricing rules, your past winning proposals, your service documentation.
- **Constrained to one job.** It drafts quotes. It does not also write blog posts.
- **Consistent in output format.** Every result comes back in the same structure, so downstream systems and people can rely on it.
- **Often connected to your systems.** Reads from the CRM, writes back to it, or hands off to another process.

The user experience difference is the whole point: instead of a rep composing a 200-word prompt, they select an account and click a button.

## The four agents that pay for themselves fastest

### 1. The quote and proposal agent

**What it does:** takes a set of requirements and produces a first-draft quote or proposal grounded in your actual catalog, pricing rules, and prior winning documents.

**Who it fits:** manufacturers, distributors, and any company with real product complexity and repeated proposal formats. Very common in this region. Cincinnati and NKY have a dense base of manufacturers and B2B distributors where reps spend hours per week assembling documents.

**Typical build:** $12,000 to $30,000, depending on catalog complexity and pricing rule logic.

**Why it pays:** proposal turnaround time is one of the few variables that reliably affects close rate. Cutting five days to one changes outcomes, not just workload.

### 2. The account intelligence agent

**What it does:** on demand, assembles a briefing on any account, internal history from the CRM, recent public news, relationship map, open items, suggested talking points.

**Who it fits:** any B2B sales team with more than about eight reps and accounts that require preparation.

**Typical build:** $8,000 to $18,000.

**Why it pays:** it is the highest-adoption agent type because it removes work reps dislike and changes nothing about how they sell.

### 3. The RFP and questionnaire agent

**What it does:** answers repeated questionnaire items (security reviews, vendor onboarding forms, RFP sections) from a maintained library of approved answers, flagging anything it is not confident about for human review.

**Who it fits:** companies selling into healthcare, financial services, government, or large enterprises. Common for NKY logistics and life sciences suppliers.

**Typical build:** $10,000 to $25,000.

**Why it pays:** the work is high-volume, low-judgment, and currently eats senior people's time. The flag-for-review behavior is what makes it safe.

### 4. The lead triage agent

**What it does:** reads inbound leads, enriches them, scores against your actual closed-won patterns, routes them, and drafts a first response.

**Who it fits:** companies with meaningful inbound volume and inconsistent response times.

**Typical build:** $8,000 to $20,000.

**Why it pays:** speed to first response is one of the most reliable predictors of inbound conversion, and most SMBs are slow at it not from indifference but from routing friction.

## What it actually costs

| Component | Range |
|---|---|
| Discovery and workflow design | $2,000 to $5,000 |
| Build and integration | $6,000 to $25,000 |
| Testing and refinement | $1,500 to $5,000 |
| Training and rollout | $2,000 to $6,000 |
| **Total build** | **$8,000 to $40,000** |
| Ongoing model/API usage | $50 to $800/month |
| Maintenance and iteration | $150 to $700/month |

The number people miss is maintenance. Your catalog changes. Your pricing changes. A rep finds an edge case. An agent that nobody owns degrades over about six months into something people stop trusting. Budget for the ownership, internal or external.

## When you should not build

**When an off-the-shelf tool already does it.** Meeting transcription, email drafting, generic content. These are solved products. Building your own is a hobby, not a business decision.

**When the workflow happens fewer than about 20 times a month.** The build cost will not amortize. Use a saved prompt.

**When your underlying data is a mess.** An agent grounded in a CRM with 35% duplicates produces confident errors. Fix the data first, or scope the first project as the cleanup.

**When nobody will own it.** If there is no named person responsible for the agent after launch, do not start.

**When it is your first AI project.** Custom builds should follow a team that already trusts AI from simpler wins. Leading with the most complex thing is how you burn credibility on the expensive project.

## How a build should be sequenced

**Weeks 1 to 2: workflow design.** Map the current process step by step, define exactly what the agent does and where the human stays in the loop, and write down the acceptance criteria, what "good output" means, specifically.

**Weeks 3 to 5: build and ground.** Connect the data sources, build the logic, iterate on output quality against real historical examples. Testing against last quarter's real work is the only meaningful quality test.

**Week 6: pilot with 3 to 5 users.** Real work, close support, daily feedback. Expect the first version to be about 70% right and the pilot to fix most of the rest.

**Weeks 7 to 8: rollout and training.** Hands-on, on real accounts, with the manager present.

**Day 90: measure.** Usage rate, output acceptance rate (how often is the draft used with minimal edits), and the business metric you named in week 1.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a custom AI agent?**
A purpose-built assistant configured for one specific workflow and grounded in your own data, catalog, pricing, past proposals, CRM records. It does one job repeatedly without being re-explained.

**How much does a custom AI agent cost to build?**
$8,000 to $25,000 for a focused single-workflow agent in the Cincinnati market, plus $200 to $1,500/month to operate. Complex multi-system agents run $25,000 to $60,000.

**How long does it take?**
Six to eight weeks from discovery to rollout for a focused agent. Longer if data cleanup is required first.

**Do we own it afterward?**
You should. Insist that prompts, configurations, and integration credentials are documented and transferred, and that your team can operate it without the builder. Agents only the builder can maintain are a liability.

**What happens when the underlying models change?**
Well-built agents are relatively portable, but they do need periodic re-testing when a model version changes. This is part of what the maintenance budget covers, and it is a real ongoing obligation rather than a theoretical one.
